Splines explained: basis functions, knots, and control points

A cubic B-spline written as a weighted sum of local basis functions.

A spline is a piecewise polynomial joined smoothly at points called knots. Rather than writing separate polynomial pieces, it's convenient to represent the whole curve as one formula: f(x) = Σi βi Bi(x), a weighted sum of fixed basis functions Bi(x). Each Bi(x) is a cubic polynomial piece that is nonzero over only a small span of x (its "local support") — that's the key structural difference from a plain polynomial term like x7, which is nonzero (and influential) across the entire domain.
